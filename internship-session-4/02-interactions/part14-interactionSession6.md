# Internship Discussion - 06: Power Grid Internship, Documentation & Project Thinking

## 1. Student Project Overview: Power Grid Corporation Internship

### Internship Details

* Shalini is doing an internship with **Power Grid Corporation of India Limited (PGCIL)**.
* The internship is based on studying electrical equipment used in power systems.

### Project Focus

The team is studying:

* Different equipment used in PGCIL.
* How each equipment functions.
* Equipment consistency and reliability.
* How equipment can be maintained safely.
* How equipment protection can help safeguard transformers and other systems.

### Team Structure

* The project team consists of **four members**.
* Team members should regularly discuss:

  * Project goals.
  * Progress made.
  * Learnings.
  * Challenges.

---

## 2. Define Learning Goals Before Starting Work

Before beginning an internship activity or field visit:

Discuss with your team:

* What do we want to learn?
* What knowledge should we gain?
* What outcomes do we expect?

Every activity should have a clear learning objective.

---

## 3. Importance of Documentation and Note-Taking

Students should maintain detailed notes because:

* Data and observations are the foundation of learning.
* Internship experiences become valuable future references.
* Notes help remember:

  * What was learned.
  * Questions that arose.
  * How doubts were solved.
  * New ideas discovered.

### Record:

* Equipment details.
* Observations.
* Questions.
* Answers received from experts.
* Technical learning.

**Key lesson:** Do not take internship learning casually; document everything.

---

## 4. Choosing a Good Project Idea

### A Project Should Solve a Real Need

* Do not create a product only because you want to make something.
* A useful product should solve someone's problem.
* Ask:

  * Who needs this solution?
  * Who would use or buy it?
  * What problem does it solve?

---

## 5. Think Beyond Your Engineering Branch

Modern projects are interdisciplinary.

A successful product may require:

* Electrical engineering.
* Mechanical engineering.
* Software development.
* Manufacturing.
* Production.
* Civil engineering.
* Metallurgy.
* Chemical engineering.
* Computer science.

### Example:

Large projects like space missions require contributions from multiple engineering disciplines.

**Key idea:** Do not limit yourself only to your own branch.

---

## 6. Developing a Project Idea

Follow this approach:

### Step 1: Identify a Problem

* Clearly define the problem you want to solve.

### Step 2: Find the Solution

* Once the problem is clearly understood, solutions become easier to identify.

### Step 3: Build a Team

Identify:

* Required expertise.
* Team members.
* Technical skills needed.

### Step 4: Create a Business Model Canvas (BMC)

Use BMC to understand:

* The value of your solution.
* Users/customers.
* Resources required.
* Implementation strategy.

---

## 7. Transformer Research Opportunity

Since the internship involves transformers, focus on understanding transformer failures.

Study:

### Problems

* Why do transformers fail?
* What are common causes of failure?

### Data Collection

Collect information such as:

* Voltage spikes.
* Operating conditions.
* Performance measurements.
* Failure patterns.
* Time-based data.

### Goal

Develop methods to:

* Predict failures.
* Prevent breakdowns.
* Improve transformer reliability.

---

## 8. Add Value During Internship

As an intern:

* Do not only observe.
* Try to generate useful insights.
* Provide meaningful data to the organization.

Example:

* If you collect valuable transformer performance data, PGCIL experts can analyze it and use it for improvement.

---

## 9. Career Opportunities Through Problem Solving

* Organizations value students who identify real problems and create solutions.
* A practical solution during an internship can create future employment opportunities.
* Large organizations require skilled people who can solve operational challenges.

---

## Important Lesson

A successful internship is about active learning, not just completing tasks. Set clear goals, document observations, collect data, identify real problems, and think beyond your engineering branch. The best projects come from understanding real-world needs and developing practical solutions through interdisciplinary teamwork.
