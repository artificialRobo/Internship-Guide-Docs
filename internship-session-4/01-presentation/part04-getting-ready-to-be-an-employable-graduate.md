# Developing the Right Mindset for Career Success

Technical knowledge alone is not enough to become employable. Students should develop a growth-oriented mindset that helps them identify opportunities, solve problems, and continuously learn.

---

## 1. Abundance Mindset

The instructor emphasized adopting an **abundance mindset** (thinking in terms of growth and opportunities rather than limitations).

### Example: Internet Evolution

* In the 3G era:

  * Internet bandwidth was limited.
  * Videos were of low quality.
* With the transition to **5G**:

  * Bandwidth increased dramatically.
  * Internet costs decreased.
  * Usage and demand grew rapidly.

**Lesson:** When resources become abundant, innovation and growth accelerate. Students should think about creating opportunities instead of focusing on scarcity.

---

## 2. Customer-Centric Thinking

  <img src="assets/customer-centric.png" alt="" width="auto">
</p>

During an internship, students should identify who their "customer" is.

For interns:

* The customer is often:

  * Their supervisor
  * Their mentor
  * Their manager

Students should continuously ask:

* What does my supervisor need?
* What problems are they trying to solve?
* How can I help them achieve their goals?

Understanding customer needs is key to delivering value.

---

## 3. Ask Questions Continuously

The instructor stressed that learning comes from curiosity.

Students should:

* Ask questions regularly.
* Clarify doubts.
* Seek deeper understanding instead of remaining silent.

The more thoughtful questions you ask, the more you learn.

---

## 4. Form and Test Hypotheses

Students should develop the habit of:

1. Making assumptions or hypotheses about a problem.
2. Testing those ideas.
3. Evaluating the results.

If an idea does not work:

* Change direction (pivot).
* Develop a new hypothesis.
* Continue experimenting until a solution is found.

This iterative approach is fundamental to innovation and problem-solving.

---

## 5. Look for Unmet Needs

Always observe your surroundings and identify problems that have not yet been solved.

Ask yourself:

* What needs are currently unmet?
* Which problems can I solve?
* How can I create value for others?

Recognizing unmet needs often leads to innovation and new opportunities.

---

## 6. Find a Mentor

<p align="center">
  <img src="assets/mentor.jpg" alt="" width="auto">
</p>

Students should have a **mentor (guide)** who can:

* Provide direction.
* Share experience.
* Offer constructive feedback.
* Help navigate challenges during internships and career development.

Learning from an experienced mentor accelerates personal and professional growth.

---

## Importance Lessons

* Develop an abundance mindset that focuses on opportunities and growth.
* During internships, think from the perspective of your supervisor or customer.
* Ask questions frequently to enhance learning.
* Build, test, and refine ideas through hypotheses and experimentation.
* Continuously look for unmet needs and opportunities to solve problems.
* Seek guidance from mentors to support your learning and career development.

---

## Quick Summary

An employable graduate develops an abundance mindset, understands customer needs, asks thoughtful questions, experiments with ideas, identifies unmet problems, and learns continuously with the guidance of mentors.
